The running order

What happens, roughly in this order

Nothing here is rigid. The order shifts with who turned up and what the room wants, and that improvised quality is the point — but this is the shape it usually takes.

01

Setup and checkin 6:45 pm

The house band sets up the backline. Coordinate with Nate for sign-in. If you're here for the jam, no worries if you miss the sign-in. You'll get a chance to sit in later.

About 45 minutes
02

The house band opens 7:30 pm

The Turnups work out a short set, about half an hour, and kick the night off. It also doubles as the sound check for everything that follows.

About 30 minutes
03

Main event starts at 8 pm, ends roughly at 10 pm

•

Guest bands

Full groups play short sets — covers, their own material, or both. A band changeover means swapping instruments and resetting a few channels, so guest sets get grouped where possible rather than scattered through the night.

Short sets, back to back

Soloists take a turn

Some play to a backing track off a phone or laptop, plugged into the board. Others ask the house band to back them — see below for how to make that easy.

A song or two each

Open Jam

The part that makes it an open jam rather than an open mic. Players who came alone get put together with whoever else is up, and the house band fills in the gaps.

Getting on the list

How to get a slot

Sign up here as early as possible, especially if you plan on playing a formal setlist. Some nights are light, some are really busy. We can forecast your needs better the further in advance we known.

If online forms are not your thing, we get it. You can reach out to us on You can reach out to us at the Shoreline Brewery Open Jam Facebook page or email directly at admin@superjamm.live.

Worst case you can just show up and we'll figure a way to get you to play, especially if you're doing solo requiring no support, or willing to play whatever comes up in the open jam.

The following are details specific to the types of performers we normally get:

  • 01
    Bands — Band sets need to be planned around changeovers, so this is the one that really needs advanced notice if you want quality play time or support from the house band.
  • 02
    Soloists — We'll need to know if you're bringing a backing track or backing from the house band.
  • 03
    Walk-ins — just tell the house band you want to play. That's the whole process. If you are locked into a specific repertoire, are a jam newcomer, or beginning musician, it is better to sign up early - the house band and regulars will help you out.

If you want the house band to back you, name the song and the key when you sign up. They can cover a lot, but the answer is much faster — and much more likely to be yes — when they've seen the request before you're already on stage.*

Gear

What's provided, and what to bring

Provided — PA

The venue's system, indoor or outdoor depending on the season.

Provided — backline

Guitar and bass amps, drums, mics, mixer, and DI. All of it belongs to the house band.

Bring — your instrument

Guitar, bass, keyboard, horn, whatever you play. Plus sticks if you're particular about them.

Optional — your own rig

Pedalboards and amps are welcome if you'd rather use yours.

Optional — a backing track

On a phone or laptop with a headphone or USB-C out. Have it cued before you're up.

Not needed — a PA, stands, or cables

Already handled.

What happens before, what happens after?

Forecast

We'll try to forecast the events as early as practical.

As of now, we'll accept sign ups up to a month in advance. You'll see on the Events page, as well as links to the forecasted event details, and recaps of previous events.

As we get request details, we'll post on the associated date event pages so interested parties can check. For signups that want to remain anonyomous we'll honor that by not sharing names or contact details.

This will be a good place to check for open jam requests, and potential for networking.

Recap

After each event we'll post short recaps, including who playing, and maybe some video too. We'll check with who plays - those who want to remain anonymous, we'll make sure your contact details don't show on the list. You can also specify if you want to remain anonymous if you are using the sign up form

First time

If you've never been

Come a bit early. The setup happens in the open, so it's an easy time to find the house band and say what you'd like to do. Tell them your instrument and whether you have something specific you want to play — that's enough for them to slot you in.

You don't need a set worked out, and you don't need to be "good". The house band are working musicians who spend their week adapting to other people's playing; sitting in with someone new is the normal case, not a favor.

It is typical to have regulars that proficient musicians, they'll jump at the chance to help you out too.

You can reach out to us on You can reach out to us at the Shoreline Brewery Open Jam Facebook page or email directly at admin@superjamm.live.

The uncertainty is the fun part. Figuring out on the fly what to play with people you've just met is what makes it a jam instead of a recital.
