=== block

^ The running order

## What happens, roughly in this order

Nothing here is rigid. The order shifts with who turned up and what the room
wants, and that improvised quality is the point — but this is the shape it
usually takes.

::: steps
### Setup and checkin 6:45 pm
The house band sets up the backline.

Coordinate with Nate for sign-in.
If you're here for the jam, no worries if you miss the sign-in.
You'll get a chance to sit in later.
@ About 45 minutes

### The house band opens 7:30 pm
*The Turnups* work out a short set, about half an hour, and kick the night off.
It also doubles as the sound check for everything that follows.
@ About 30 minutes

### Main event starts at 8 pm, ends roughly at 10 pm

#### Guest bands
Full groups play short sets — covers, their own material, or both. A band
changeover means swapping instruments and resetting a few channels, so guest
sets get grouped where possible rather than scattered through the night.
@ Short sets, back to back

#### Soloists take a turn
Some play to a backing track off a phone or laptop, plugged into the board.
Others ask the house band to back them — see below for how to make that easy.
@ A song or two each

#### Open Jam
The part that makes it an open jam rather than an open mic. Players who came
alone get put together with whoever else is up, and the house band fills in the
gaps.
@ Until the night ends
:::

=== block

^ Getting on the list

## How to get a slot

::: numbers
1. **Bands** — get in touch ahead of time. Band sets need to be planned around changeovers, so this is the one that really wants notice.
2. **Soloists** — sign up on the night, or ahead of time once the signup form is up. Say whether you're bringing a backing track or want the house band behind you.
3. **Walk-ins** — just tell the house band you want to play. That's the whole process.
:::

::: callout
If you want the house band to back you, name the song *and the key* when you
sign up. They can cover a lot, but the answer is much faster — and much more
likely to be yes — when they've seen the request before you're already on
stage.*
:::

=== block

^ Gear

## What's provided, and what to bring

::: cards
- **Provided — PA** — The venue's system, indoor or outdoor depending on the season.
- **Provided — backline** — Guitar and bass amps, keys, drums, mics, mixer, and DI. All of it belongs to the house band.
- **Bring — your instrument** — Guitar, bass, horn, whatever you play. Plus sticks if you're particular about them.
- **Optional — your own rig** — Pedalboards and amps are welcome if you'd rather use yours.
- **Optional — a backing track** — On a phone or laptop with a headphone or USB-C out. Have it cued before you're up.
- **Not needed — a PA, stands, or cables** — Already handled.
:::

=== block

^ First time

## If you've never been

Come a bit early. The setup happens in the open, so it's an easy time to find
the house band and say what you'd like to do. Tell them your instrument and
whether you have something specific you want to play — that's enough for them
to slot you in.

You don't need a set worked out, and you don't need to be good. The house band
are working musicians who spend their week adapting to other people's playing;
sitting in with someone new is the normal case, not a favour.

::: kicker
The uncertainty is the fun part. Figuring out on the fly what to play with
people you've just met is what makes it a jam instead of a recital.
:::
