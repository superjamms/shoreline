# README

This project is set up to enhance the Shoreline Brewery Open Jam online presence.

There are a couple of perceived problems:

- Online presence is almost non-existent
- Unrealized partnership opportunities between venue, house band, sponsors and guest bands

The actual open events are a lot of fun, individually are run well, but could be done better.

**The plan lives in [PLAYBOOK.md](PLAYBOOK.md)** — weekly events, signups, and setlist
tracking, with a phased rollout.

**The public pages live in [site/](site/README.md)** — Markdown in `site/content/`,
rendered by `site/build.py` into `docs/`, which GitHub Pages serves off `main`.
Five pages — *Home*, *Events*, *How it works*, *People* and *About* — plus one
page per Tuesday under `site/content/events/`, which the home page and the events
page build their lists from. Styling follows the brewery's own site (orange and
navy off the bowtie logo, free stand-ins for their Adobe Fonts).

## What works well

### House band and backline

The House Band includes seasoned *working* musicians:

- Multi-instrumentalists
- Gig across one or more bands, or do solo performances
- Know and use functional theory
- Adapt well to the needs of whoever is sitting in.

Bonus points they work together and teach at a local music store.

The gear is modern, and it comes from two places. The **venue provides the PA**. Everything
else — guitar and bass amps, keys, drums, mics, mixer and bass/guitar FX/amp/cab DI — is
**provided by the house band**, in practice by the coordinator and the bass player personally.
That gear is hauled in, set up, torn down and hauled back out every Tuesday by the same two
people. Worth stating plainly on both counts: neither contribution is visible anywhere online,
and the weekly teardown is a real constraint — nothing can be left permanently installed, so
anything we settle about the setup has to survive being packed into a car each week.

This means sit-ins only need to show up, and bring an instrument not already provided by the backline.
That said, events can accommodate when sit-ins want to use their own FX boards and/or amps.

It is also a plus that the backline supports wiring in backing tracks for solo acts.

### Partners

There are a few implicit partners, although not clear from online presence

- *Shoreline* - visibly the lead partner as the hosting venue, and provides the PA
- *House Band* - the other visible partner coordinating and hosting each open jam event, and supplying the entire backline out of their own gear
- *Roxy Music* - some of the guest bands are via Roxy Music student programs - the house band members work and teach at Roxy, there is clearly an implicit partner relationship, although not clear via online presence
- *Guest Bands* - some other guest bands may show, or players from those bands (New Groove, Catalyst, etc)

### Regulars

There are recurring regulars that give a sense of familiarity and continuity for the jams.

They have a rapport with the house band - it is common to see some good natured trash talk,
but also welcoming to all, especially new comers.

### Venue

- Location downtown Michigan City
- Shoreline has a nice vibe - facilities are clean, food and service quality are very good.
- The venue coordinator also happens to work the bar, knows the customers intimately, and works directly with the house band.
- The venue runs separate indoor and outdoor PA systems, for seasonal use. This is not
  something an attendee needs to know and shouldn't go in public copy, but it does need
  settling between the venue and house band coordinators ahead of a given night — it changes
  where the band's mixer patches in and how long setup takes.

## Types of sets

The setup is versatile - it functions as both an Open Mic (soloist) and Open Jam (Collaborative Jam) event.
It gives a venue that can attract different artists, performers, musicians with different goals.

There's a pattern to who plays on a given night. Four rough categories, and they
want different things:

1. **The house band opens** — the Turn Ups work out a short set, about half an
   hour, and kick off the night
2. **One or more guest bands sit in** for short sets, playing covers or their own material and
   often bringing some of their own following with them
3. **Soloists** — Regulars may sit in, showcasing their own work. Some play to a backing track, some request house band coverage
4. **People who want to play with others** — bringing some of their own
   repertoire, or just there to jam

These are worth separating because the needs are different.
- Each guest band may need prep or changes to the backline between sets
- A soloist on a backing track needs their own device connected to the board, and disconnected again after.
- Someone who just wants to jam needs to know what the room can play and who else is up — that's the one group nothing currently serves.

Why any of them come is their own business, and the nights are a lot of fun
regardless.

But from a coordinator's chair there's an obvious quid pro quo sitting unused with types 2 and 3: they bring people through the door, and the page could be putting them in front of its own audience afterwards. Cross-promotion is worth doing whether or not anyone showed up wanting it.

## What could be better

### Coordination and planning

Not transparent:

- *No setlist / songlist tracking* — newcomers have no idea what is typically played, or whether the house band (or regulars) can cover their requests
- *No signup list* — while guest band slots scheduling may be done directly through coordinator or house band members, but soloists, regulars and walk-ins will not know when they play, or if the house band or regulars can back them.
- *Attendance inconsistency* - Some nights are idle (leaves a lot of open space), while others are packed (coordinating solo and band spots can spin time)

The inconsistency and the lack of a record compound each other:

- **It does not build traction.** Whatever was worked out this week is gone by next week,
  so the same questions get answered from scratch
- **It's single-threaded.** The plan lives with whoever was in the thread (coordinator/house band members). If they
  aren't there, neither is the information

Writing it down would do two jobs, not one: it preserves the planning that does
happen, and it makes the weeks where it didn't happen *visible*. An empty sheet on
Sunday is itself the signal to do something about it — which is a thing no amount of
private conversation can give you.

There is another side-effect of the signup and songlist - it gives a way to get a potential soloist feedback whether the house band can back them,
or if they should bring their own backing track and device.

Caveat: While the signup and song lists add structure, they are not intended to replace the improv element, especially with walk-ins.
The uncertainty element, having players figure out in the moment what to play on the fly, is what makes the open jam vibe.

### More exposure

- Advocacy and marketing for open jam, house band and members, and regular bands are by
  word-of-mouth — the follower list looks stale
- No songlist or guide to what type of music is played
- Bio is very short — might be good to provide a little more info about how the open jams work
- Facebook integration (songlists/signup lists)

## Open questions

Two things worth settling before acting on the playbook, both turned up while
checking public sources:

- **The start time is inconsistent.** The bio above says 7:00 pm. Other listings
  say 7:30 pm. The venue's own open mic page says 9:30 pm. Pick one and push it
  everywhere.
- **The jam is missing from the venue's own calendar.**
  `shorelinebrewery.com/events` lists Jazz Night, bocce, and the Sunday music
  series, but not the Tuesday jam. Their `/events/openmic` page is a stale event
  dated 20 Feb 2018.
